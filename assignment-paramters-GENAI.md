## **Final Project (30%)**

Students will form groups of up to **three members** for the final project. You may work **solo** or in a **group of two or three**. Throughout the semester, each group will work on **one project**.

There are **two project tracks**, and each group must choose **one**.

### Track 1: Project / Startup Track

The group should build a project that solves a **real-world problem using AI**. The topic can be anything, as long as AI is meaningfully used. This track **requires**: a working prototype and demo, user testing and feedback, and/or deployment, if possible. The project does not need to be a scalable startup. For example, an automated generative YouTuber that earns money can also be considered a small startup-like project if appropriately tested or deployed.

The evaluation will focus primarily on the quality of the demo, user feedback and testing, and evidence of real-world use scenarios. Concept-only projects or vaporware are not acceptable. Also, a working system alone is usually not sufficient. You must deploy the system (even with N=1 or 2 users), allow real users to use it, and collect feedback.

**Rough grading criteria:**

- **30/30 (100% – A+ level):** The project actually earns money (even from a single user), *or* is deployed and attracts a substantial number of users (e.g., 50–100), *or* demonstrates other impressive real-world track records.
- **28-29/30 (95% – A level):** The project is used and tested by a meaningful number of real target users (e.g., a dozen users), with clear evidence that it solves their problem. The proposed solution is well-differentiated from existing competitors or alternatives.
- **27/30 (90% – A- level):** The project is appropriately designed based on user feedback, is fully working, and has undergone multiple user tests. It shows potential and is somewhat competitive.
- **25-26/30 (85% – B+ level):** A working project and prototype are developed. It may be too easy to implement.
- **-24/30 (80% – B level):** All other cases (e.g., the system is mostly conceptual or shows little real-world impact).

### Track 2: Academia / Research Track

In this track, the group should aim to write an **academic paper**. The topic can be anything related to AI. Possible topics or venues include: AI (e.g., NeurIPS, CVPR, ACL), HCI (e.g., CHI, UIST), Graphics (e.g., SIGGRAPH), Robotics (e.g., ICRA, IROS), or other appropriate venues. Students must follow the official publication format for the chosen venue (e.g., ACM two-column format for CHI/UIST). If you are working on your own existing research or project, you can reuse or build on top of your own work. The topic can be anything—from a system implementation, new algorithms or models for a certain benchmark, providing datasets, studying and evaluating through experiments, or any other topics. 

Each group should:

- Clearly state the novelty and contributions
- Conduct a literature review (at least 30 citations)
- Describe the outcome and discuss the results

Grading will be primarily based on an academic review process. I will ask others (e.g., PhD students or faculty) to review your paper and provide 1–5 rating scores (3–3.5 = borderline accept, 2.5 = borderline reject). Attach one short review (2–3 paragraphs). You can also ask one of your colleagues (e.g., PhD students or faculty) to provide a review, too. In this case, the review needs to be honest. 

**Rough grading criteria:**

- **30/30 (100% – A+ level):** Two reviewers (one of them being me) believe the paper has potential for acceptance at a target venue, either now or in the future. The direction is highly promising, and the actual submission after the class is realistic.
- **28-29/30 (95% – A level):** One reviewer (either me or another reviewer) believes the paper has potential for future acceptance. The direction is promising.
- **27/30 (90% – A– level):** The work demonstrates a strong understanding of the literature and addresses a meaningful problem. It is potentially submittable to arXiv.
- **25-26/30 (85% – B+ level):** The paper has a basic academic structure. It may not be a significant contribution.
- **-24/30 (80% – B level):** Other cases (e.g., weak literature understanding, does not resemble an academic paper).

### **Project Deliverables**

Each group must submit the following:

1. **Writing PDF**
    - **Project / Startup Track**: Any format is acceptable. The document should describe project goals, design and development process, visuals (sketches, illustrations), and references if applicable.
    - **Academia / Research Track**: Must strictly follow the format of the target venue (e.g., IEEE two-column for CVPR, ACM two-column for CHI/UIST). The section structure should follow venue conventions (e.g., Abstract, Introduction, Related Work, System, User Study, Discussion, Limitations, Future Work, References).
2. **Video:** A recorded demo video (project track) or presentation video (research track).
3. **Slides PDF:** A presentation including visuals and videos, presented in class. Presentation length is about 5–10 minutes, depending on the total number of teams.
    - **Project / Startup Track**: Focus first on the demo (live or video), then user evidence and feedback
    - **Academia / Research Track**: Focus first on contributions and novelty, especially compared to the closest related work

### Grading Criteria and Rubrics

1. **For Project / Startup Track:**
    - Quality of the demo and system
    - Evidence of real-world problem-solving (e.g., user feedback, track record, revenue, etc)
    - Understanding and comparison with existing competitors or alternatives
2. **For Academic / Research Track:**
    - Novelty and originality of the idea
    - Significance of contributions
    - Understanding and comparison with existing research
